Subject-Scriptorium Distinction

The Dossier does not keep armies. It keeps faces that bend armies.

The Annals carry the warband's full burden: units, engines, relics, battles, wounds, and doctrines. The Dossier is narrower and more dangerous. It receives only names that have become pressure points.

A subject is admitted when its presence changes other records. A commander alters formations. A relic-bearer alters the meaning of a weapon. A rival alters the shape of future hate. A specialist alters the army's method of survival.

The Dossier therefore refuses clean biography. It preserves recurrence, function, contradiction, and the moment a name becomes too useful to leave unchained.

Subject-Scriptorium Procedure

A living name is not written. It is restrained until it explains itself.

Each subject enters through fragments: battlefield rumor, weapon trace, repeated witness phrase, impossible survival, relic association, or the discomfort of nearby records changing shape.

The Dossier does not correct contradictions too early. Contradictions are often the first signs that a subject is still becoming useful.

I

Acquisition

The name arrives by rumor, relic, corpse-tag, scrap-vox, command notice, or battlefield recurrence.

II

Comparison

The name is weighed against the Annals, the War-Chronicles, and existing relic traces.

III

Fermentation

Contradictions remain sealed together until a pattern begins to breathe beneath them.

IV

Custody

Only then does the Dossier grant a chamber, and even then the subject may continue corrupting its own record.
