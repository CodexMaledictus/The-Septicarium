DATAVAULT PURPOSE

INTERNAL RECORD

What the Dossier Is

The Plaguebound Dossier is the Septicarium’s registry of those who serve, infect, command, contradict, endure, and transform beneath the shadow of Mortivus Classarion.

It records plaguebound figures not by rank alone, but by devotional pressure: what they survived, what they spread, what they lost, what they became, and what the archive suspects they are becoming.

Principle I Rank is less reliable than symptom.
Principle II Contradiction is not an error. It is often the first mark of favor.
Principle III Witness accounts are not corrected. They are allowed to decay beside one another.

DATAVAULT LORE

CENSURED HISTORY

Why It Was Built

The first Plaguebound Dossier was not written by a scribe. It was assembled from failed orders, corrupted vox returns, stained armor manifests, plague-serf confessions, and the muttering of censers hung too close to shipboard ventilation shrines.

Mortivus permitted the archive to continue because it served a purpose beyond memory. It watched the host from within. It tracked which warriors merely obeyed and which had begun to ripen toward stranger stations.

Over time the chamber ceased to be a roster and became an omen-engine. It does not only ask who serves the Vanguard. It asks who is nearest to becoming something the current host is not yet prepared to name aloud.

How a Name Becomes Preserved

A subject does not enter the Dossier as a completed entity. He is pulled through wound, witness, contradiction, recurrence, and omen until the chamber judges the name worthy of preservation.

THE SEVEN CONDITIONS

RACK LAW

A name must rot through seven permissions before the Dossier will keep it.

I
Witness

The name must be seen.

A subject begins as rumor, stain, casualty note, or damaged vox utterance.

II
Wound

The name must suffer mark.

Unwounded names are too dry. The archive prefers subjects opened by consequence.

III
Contradiction

The name must disagree with itself.

Clean agreement suggests the subject is not yet interesting enough to preserve.

IV
Recurrence

The name must return.

A single mention is evidence. Repetition is infection.

V
Symptom

The name must alter those nearby.

True subjects leave pressure, fear, loyalty, fever, or devotion around them.

VI
Omen

The name must lean forward.

Some names behave as if their futures have already begun pulling on them.

VII
Tally

The name must be kept open.

Closure is a clean habit. The Dossier preserves what continues to worsen.
