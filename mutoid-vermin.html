Mutoid Vermin

Infestation Registry of the Plague Host

Mutoid Vermin are not one beast. They are the sacred vermin-cluster of the Death Guard and the Gellerpox Infected: Glitchlings, Cursemites, Eyestinger Swarms, and Sludge-Grubs. To hostile archivists, they are contagion organisms. To The Rotfather's Vanguard, they are the little mouths of abundance, blessed carriers of holy rot, and the living proof that no form of life is too low to be crowned by decay.

They are recorded here as biology, weapon, omen, and scripture: a chittering theology that eats through flesh, machinery, breath, discipline, memory, and the lie that cleanliness can ever be permanent.

Opening Archive Statement

The Vermin Are a Gospel Written Below the Boot.

The Imperial record calls them mutant pests. The Vanguard knows better. Vermin endure beneath empires. Vermin survive where banners fall. Vermin enter sealed places by routes pride never bothers to guard.

Record / Scripture Interface

The Mutoid Vermin represent a unique intersection between biological pathology and daemonic intrusion. They are neither pure daemon nor merely animal. They are liminal, quasi-daemonic symbionts: fed by flesh, sustained by rot, and carried forward by the ambient blessing of the plague god.

Within the holds of The Rotfather's Vanguard, these creatures are not treated as accidental infestation. They are allowed to breed in sump-darkness, corridor seams, plague vents, rusted shrine machinery, and old blood. Their presence marks a vessel as fertile. Their chittering is read as a low hymn. Their hunger is not an embarrassment to war. It is war reduced to its most honest grammar.

Where the warrior brings doctrine, the vermin bring inevitability. Where the bolter speaks once, the swarm speaks forever.

The four strains form a complete infestation liturgy: the Cursemite enters the body; the Sludge-Grub sanctifies waste; the Eyestinger conquers the air; the Glitchling teaches the machine to rot.

Biological Origin / Pathogenesis

How the Vermin Were Made Holy.

They were not engineered. They were not summoned in the traditional sense. They were ordinary organisms that crossed a threshold no one thought to close.

Origin Record
Warp Breach and Gellerpox Vector

The Threshold No One Sealed.

The Mutoid Vermin did not begin in the warp. They began in the dark spaces between walls, in the bilge of long-haul transports, in the forgotten corners of hive cities where no one carries a torch. They were mundane organisms: parasitic, opportunistic, ecologically suited to decay.

The Gellerpox changed them. Carried by those exposed to corrupted Geller fields during warp transit, the Gellerpox virus does not merely mutate its hosts—it creates a new biological attractor. Organisms that feed near Gellerpox-infected tissue begin to absorb trace warp-saturation. Over successive generations, this exposure produces hyper-evolution: bodies that no longer follow standard biological logic.

The result is something the Mechanicus has no clean category for: not wholly daemonic, not wholly natural. Quasi-daemonic. Liminal. Sustained by rot-energy and ambient plague-blessing, able to survive in conditions that would sterilize a standard organism in seconds.

Stage I

Exposure

Ordinary vermin feeding near Gellerpox hosts. Warp-trace absorbed through contaminated tissue.

Stage II

Hyper-Evolution

Accelerated generational mutation collapses a century of adaptation into weeks. Bodies become wrong.

Stage III

Quasi-Daemonic State

Warp-saturation achieves symbiosis. The organism is now partially sustained by the Plague God's ambient will.

Stage IV

Canonical Strain Lock

Four stable Mutoid phenotypes emerge. The biology has found its four blessed offices and holds them.

Battlefield Function / War Doctrine

The Vermin Do Not Fight. They Precede the Fight.

Each strain holds a distinct battlefield office. Together, they form a living disruption layer that the Death Guard deploys before, during, and after conventional engagement.

War Record
Death Guard Synergy / Gellerpox Affinity

Living Disruption. Holy Disorder.

Plague Marines do not need the Mutoid Vermin to function. But the Mutoid Vermin amplify every plague doctrine the Death Guard deploys. They are not a weapon in the conventional sense. They are a condition—an environmental state that plague warriors move through freely while enemies drown in.

Cursemites are released into enclosed spaces hours before assault, latching to defenders and draining focus and strength before a single bolter is raised. Sludge-Grubs are allowed to proliferate in supply lines and water sources, converting defender logistics into poison. Eyestinger Swarms are released into vox-relay stations, command tents, and sniper positions—anywhere precision and communication matter. Glitchlings are seeded into machinery during infiltration phases, ensuring that when the assault begins, no enemy weapon functions with full confidence.

Together, the four strains do not defeat an enemy. They convince the enemy that the war is already over. By the time the warriors arrive, only surrender or transformation remains.

Infestation Escalation

Three Stages of Blessed Occupation.

A Mutoid Vermin infestation does not peak and crash like a conventional pest outbreak. It escalates toward a stable holy state from which it does not return.

Escalation Chronology
Stage I

Initial Contact

The first vermin are rarely seen. Cursemite traces appear—small welts, unexplained blood loss. Machines stutter without clear cause. The host population attributes these signs to fatigue, mechanical wear, and superstition. The infestation does not announce itself. It waits.

Stage II

Colony Establishment

The swarms become visible. Eyestingers cloud vents and corridors. Sludge-Grubs appear in drainage systems. Glitchlings are spotted in cable runs. Extermination attempts fail because the colonies have already found the sealed places—the walls inside the walls, the dark beneath the dark—where no fumigation reaches.

Stage III

Terminal Symbiosis

The vermin and the structure become one. Sludge-Grubs process all organic waste into viable colony nutrition. Glitchlings have fully integrated with the machine-spirit of the facility. Eyestingers have established permanent breeding chambers. The Cursemites have moved from feeding to cultivating. The place is no longer infested. It has become something else entirely.

Outbreak Records / Addenda

Case Files from the Blessed Contamination.

Compiled from Septicarium field observations, intercepted Administratum quarantine reports, and testimony extracted from survivors who wished they had not survived.

Sealed Records
Outbreak Record I — The Tannery District, Hive Tertullian

The first verified Mutoid Vermin colony outside a Death Guard vessel was documented within a tanning district of Hive Tertullian's lower sublevels. The colony emerged from what sanitation records described as a routine organic overflow event—a burst sump pipe in a leather-processing facility that had been operational for three centuries without incident.

Within four weeks of the overflow, tannery workers reported unusual insect activity: creatures that did not resemble any catalogued species, moving in non-standard patterns, congregating around living hosts rather than organic waste. Cursemite activity was identified first, followed by Sludge-Grub proliferation in the drainage system.

The district was quarantined forty-six days after first contact. By that point, the Glitchlings had already compromised the facility's cogitator network. The quarantine seals did not function as designed. The district is listed in Administratum records as Condemned, Tertullian Sub-Level Eleven. The Vanguard's liturgical record calls it the First Tannery Chapel.

Outbreak Record II — Xenos Rejection Data, Exodite Contact

Documented field observation from the Rotfather's Vanguard's engagement near the Exodite world of Verenath's Rest. The Mutoid Vermin were deployed as an advance contamination layer ahead of the main assault force. Standard doctrine.

Anomaly noted: the Eyestinger Swarms did not colonize the Exodite settlements as expected. The native flora appeared to produce a biochemical compound that the swarms found hostile. The Cursemites similarly showed reduced latching efficiency against Exodite hosts. Current hypothesis is that the Eldar's psycho-spiritual connection to their world creates a biological environment partially resistant to warp-saturated organisms. The Sludge-Grubs showed no such inhibition.

This remains one of the only documented cases of partial Mutoid Vermin combat ineffectiveness. The Vanguard's Plague Surgeons do not regard this as failure. They regard it as a problem worth cultivating a solution to.

Outbreak Record III — The Mechanicus Folly, Forge-Station Kethara

A Mechanicus Explorator team captured a live Glitchling specimen for study at Forge-Station Kethara. The station's Arch-Magos classified the organism as a potential research asset: a biological system demonstrating what appeared to be genuine machine-interfacing capability, unprecedented in standard xenobiology.

The Glitchling was held in a sealed cogitator-shielded containment cell. Within seventy-two hours, three additional Glitchlings were found inside the containment cell. Within six days, the station's primary cogitator array began returning corrupted data. Within twelve days, the station lost contact with its parent forge world.

Recovery teams found Forge-Station Kethara three months later. The Glitchlings had propagated through the entire station's machine-spirit network. The Arch-Magos's final log entry reads: "It was not studying us. It was breeding us a home." The station is now avoided. Strange transmissions are occasionally reported from its position.

Outbreak Record IV — Trench Folklore, Verdant Mire Campaign

Collected from survivor testimony following the Verdant Mire campaign on Opaline VII. Imperial Guard units holding trench networks for eleven months developed a specific mythology around the Mutoid Vermin activity they observed daily in their positions.

The most consistent piece of folklore: soldiers came to believe that Cursemites only attacked those who had lost the will to survive—that the creatures could smell despair. This was not biologically accurate. The Cursemites showed no preference based on psychological state. However, the belief itself had measurable morale effects. Units that understood they were being targeted by plague organisms with divine purpose broke faster than units that retained any illusion of control over their fate.

The folklore may represent the human mind's attempt to construct meaning in response to theologically constructed biological warfare. The Vanguard liturgists note with satisfaction that the soldiers were not entirely wrong. The vermin did not choose the despairing. But they did arrive as an inevitability. The soldiers understood that much correctly.

Addendum I — Black Market Specimen Trade, Underhive Provenance Unknown

Multiple reports across three segmentum trading hubs document an active black market in preserved and living Mutoid Vermin specimens. Cursemite egg clusters in particular command significant premiums among collectors of xeno-organisms, criminal guilds with interest in biological weapons, and a small but documented community of individuals who appear to revere the creatures for religious reasons not affiliated with any recognized Chaos doctrine.

The Ordo Malleus has flagged this trade as a Threat Level Three containment concern. Seven interdiction operations have been conducted. None have resulted in complete supply chain disruption. The Vanguard finds this unremarkable. Things that are holy spread by the routes that are open to them. The black market is simply another corridor without a seal.

Siphonaptera Mutatis / Cursemite

The Blessed Latch

The Cursemite is between four and twelve millimeters in length at maturity, though specimens of anomalous size have been documented in high-density colonies. The body is chitin-plated, segmented across nine joints, and equipped with six barbed attachment limbs capable of penetrating light ceramite at sustained pressure.

The proboscis is the defining organ: a hollow drilling appendage tipped with a micro-serrated bore that penetrates soft tissue and begins immediately secreting a cocktail of anticoagulant, low-grade Gellerpox trace-compound, and what Plague Surgeons classify as a "wound-keeping agent"— a biological compound that actively prevents the body's healing response from closing the entry point.

In doctrine, the Cursemite is the installation specialist. It does not cause death directly. It prepares the body to receive further rot. Every Cursemite bite is an open door. The Sludge-Grub, the Eyestinger toxin, and the Gellerpox virus all find Cursemite-prepared wounds significantly more receptive. The Cursemite bites first, and everything else enters through what it has opened.

The door does not open from the inside. The Cursemite opens it from the outside, and it stays open forever.
Vermes Putrefactio / Sludge-Grub

The Blessed Stomach

The Sludge-Grub is the largest of the four canonical strains, ranging from eight centimeters to over thirty in the oldest colony specimens. The body is soft-segmented, lacking the rigid chitin of the Cursemite, with a translucent outer membrane that allows internal organs to be observed during feeding—a feature that has made it a subject of particular revulsion in Imperial field reports and particular reverence in Vanguard liturgy.

The digestive system is the defining apparatus. The Sludge-Grub maintains three stomach chambers operating in sequence: the first breaks down organic matter; the second processes the result with a concentrated acid that can dissolve light metals; the third converts the resultant slurry into a nutrient-dense secretion that feeds colony offspring and, when released into the environment, creates conditions favorable to further infestation.

The Vanguard uses Sludge-Grubs as the colony's logistical heart. While Cursemites wound and Eyestingers disrupt, the Sludge-Grubs process everything that falls—turning the battlefield's dead into fuel for the living infestation. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is clean. Every corpse becomes colony infrastructure. The battlefield is always, eventually, a feeding table.

Blessed is the slow stomach, for it wastes nothing. All who fall become something better than what they were.
Diptera Empyrea / Eyestinger Swarm

The Blessed Wing

The Eyestinger is the only Mutoid Vermin strain that operates exclusively as a collective. Individual specimens, isolated from the swarm, exhibit drastically reduced behavior complexity. Within a swarm, however, the organisms demonstrate coordinated target-selection, distributed threat-response, and what Mechanicus observers have controversially described as "apparent group cognition."

Each individual Eyestinger carries venom sacs capable of producing a neuro-inflammatory compound targeted specifically at ocular tissue. The primary weapon is not death. It is blinding. It is respiratory distress. It is the sensation, when a swarm is encountered, that the air itself has become an opponent. Troops facing Eyestinger Swarms without adequate respirator equipment report complete combat ineffectiveness within four minutes of contact.

The swarm demonstrates a targeting preference for exposed sensory equipment—optic lenses, vox-grilles, respirator filters, scope apertures. It does not attack these components randomly. It prioritizes them. Whether this represents a form of tactical intelligence or an evolutionary attractor toward the most destructive available targets remains a subject of debate among Plague Surgeons who are not concerned enough about the answer to stop the swarms from doing it.

Blessed are those who make the enemy blind, for the blind warrior swings at saints and shadows with equal certainty.
Daemonicus Mechanica / Glitchling

The Blessed Malfunction

The Glitchling is the strain that most disturbs the Adeptus Mechanicus, and therefore the strain that most delights the Vanguard's Plague Surgeons. It is not, strictly speaking, purely biological. The Glitchling integrates living tissue and what Mechanicus analysts can only describe as "ambient machine-spirit corruption"—a warp-saturation specifically attuned to the frequencies of cogitator and machine operation rather than organic tissue.

The creature nests in cable runs, data-conduits, and shrine-cogitator housing. It does not eat the cable. It sleeps against it. The proximity of its warp-saturated body is sufficient to introduce what Mechanicus engineers document as "systemic data-corruption," "spontaneous function inversion," and "machine-spirit agitation." In plainer language: the machine begins to fail, and fails in ways that resist standard diagnosis because the cause is biological rather than mechanical.

A weapon held by a soldier operating in Glitchling-infested territory may misfire. It may fail to fire. It may fire at the wrong moment. The soldier cannot know which will happen. This uncertainty is the Glitchling's primary weapon—not the malfunction itself, but the mistrust it installs between the warrior and every tool they depend on.

Blessed is the rust that makes the trigger hesitate. In that hesitation, the Rotfather enters.
