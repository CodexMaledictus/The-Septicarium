Mutoid Vermin

Infestation Registry of the Plague Host

Mutoid Vermin are not one beast. They are the sacred vermin-cluster of the Death Guard and the Gellerpox Infected: Glitchlings, Cursemites, Eyestinger Swarms, and Sludge-Grubs. To hostile archivists, they are contagion organisms. To The Rotfather's Vanguard, they are the little mouths of abundance — blessed carriers of holy rot, and the living proof that no form of life is too low to be crowned by decay.

They are recorded here as biology, weapon, omen, and scripture: a chittering theology that eats through flesh, machinery, breath, discipline, memory, and the lie that cleanliness can ever be permanent.

Opening Archive Statement

The Vermin Are a Gospel Written Below the Boot.

The Imperial record calls them mutant pests. The Vanguard knows better. Vermin endure beneath empires. Vermin survive where banners fall. Vermin enter sealed places by routes pride never bothers to guard.

Record / Scripture Interface

The Mutoid Vermin represent a unique intersection between biological pathology and daemonic intrusion. They are neither pure daemon nor merely animal. They are liminal, quasi-daemonic symbionts: fed by flesh, sustained by rot, and carried forward by the ambient blessing of the plague god.

Within the holds of The Rotfather's Vanguard, these creatures are not treated as accidental infestation. They are allowed to breed in sump-darkness, corridor seams, plague vents, rusted shrine machinery, and old blood. Their presence marks a vessel as fertile. Their chittering is read as a low hymn. Their hunger is not an embarrassment to war. It is war reduced to its most honest grammar.

Where the warrior brings doctrine, the vermin bring inevitability. Where the bolter speaks once, the swarm speaks forever.

The four strains form a complete infestation liturgy: the Cursemite enters the body; the Sludge-Grub sanctifies waste; the Eyestinger conquers the air; the Glitchling teaches the machine to rot.

Expandable Case Files
Voidship Lower Deck Infestation

A Geller field instability turns bilge life into a multi-strain infestation. Cursemites attach to crew, Eyestingers seize the air, Sludge-Grubs colonize waste channels, and Glitchlings spoil doors, vox relays, and cogitators until the ship's own systems begin defending the outbreak. By the time the first quarantine order is issued, the lower decks are no longer crew territory. They are a warm digestive organ.

Corpse-Starch Contamination Incident

Sludge-Grub remains contaminate industrial food processing. Rations appear visually normal while carrying dormant empyric resonance. Consumption results in synchronized nightmares, delayed plague onset, and internal vermin birth across locked hab-units. The horror is not that hunger led to corruption. It is that corruption learned to look like food.

The Putrid Feast of Hive Secundus

Starving underhivers attempt to trap and cook Novitius-stage vermin. Boiling releases toxic vapors; consumption converts the digestive tract into a daemonic nursery, forcing living infant variants through the host's throat. The Vanguard records this not as punishment, but as an inverted communion: the desperate eating the blessed, and the blessed being born from within them.

Venom Cartels and Corpse-Milking

Underhive cartels extract Sludge-Grub acids and Eyestinger venom using disposable servitors. Every vial is unstable. Every buyer believes they have acquired a weapon. Most have acquired an egg.

Mechanicus Folly at Forge World Morax

Attempts to harvest Sludge-Grub digestive acids for industrial exploitation end with scrap-code accumulation, containment field reversal, Skitarii trapped in darkness, and a research sector melting into the planet's mantle. The Machine Cult called it a failure of containment. The Glitchlings called it access.

The Chitin-Armor Initiative

Tech-priests attempt to forge armor from Cursemite chitin. Initial ballistic trials succeed. Body-heat activation follows. The armor begins to parasitize the wearer, driving barbs inward and fusing itself to bone. The lesson: anything taken from the vermin still remembers how to feed.

Strategic Eradication Protocols

Purge Is Never Clean.

The Imperium answers with flame, chemical purge, pressure venting, quarantine, and orbital denial. The Vermin answer by hiding in seams, bursting after death, and teaching machines to fail.

Containment Difficulty: Extreme
Promethium / Pressure Venting / Total Denial

The only successful purge is total denial of habitat.

Promethium, plasma, pressure venting, blast-door isolation, and orbital sterilization may reduce an outbreak. They rarely end it. The Cursemite survives in cracks. The Sludge-Grub survives in runoff. The Eyestinger survives in vents. The Glitchling survives inside the system that claims the purge succeeded.

To The Rotfather's Vanguard, every eradication effort is another offering: fire that hardens survivors, pressure that spreads spores, panic that breaks discipline, machinery that opens itself from the inside.

Aeldari Revulsion

Aeldari forces avoid melee contamination and prefer molecular-level eradication. Drukhari experiments with Novitius-stage specimens end in multiplication, reality instability, and forced abandonment. Even the most depraved flesh-sculptors of Commorragh find little profit in a thing that refuses to stay owned.

Desperate Consumption and Culinary Horror

Starvation produces a special category of blasphemy. Attempts to cook, boil, render, butcher, or consume Mutoid Vermin produce vapors, blindness, hallucination, internal mutation, and living infestation-birth. The body becomes a larder that screams.

Cultist Pit-Fighting and Blood Sports

Cults use captured specimens as spectacle and recruitment. Spectators who cheer at a Cursemite opening a veteran gladiator are marked by the cult, not for what they know, but for what they enjoyed. The rot enters first through appetite.

Trench Folklore

Guardsmen invent rituals around clicks, salt, iron, smiling medics, and corpse-candle myths. Some are tactically disastrous. Others are merely the shape terror takes when command cannot explain the truth. The soldiers understood the inevitability. The rest was incorrect biology around a correct theology.

Anomalous Strain Sightings — Vox-Mimics, Rust-Weavers, Chrono-Ticks

Unconfirmed organisms that replicate crew voices, grow crystalline across bulkheads, or induce temporal confusion. Each shows a different way vermin logic can violate voice, metal, or time. The taxonomy is not finished.

Siphonaptera Mutatis / Cursemite

The Blessed Latch

Latching parasite, blood-drainer, wound-installer.

The Cursemite is between four and twelve millimeters in length at maturity, though specimens of anomalous size have been documented in high-density colonies. The body is chitin-plated, segmented across nine joints, and equipped with six barbed attachment limbs capable of penetrating light ceramite at sustained pressure.

The proboscis is the defining organ: a hollow drilling appendage tipped with a micro-serrated bore that penetrates soft tissue and begins immediately secreting a cocktail of anticoagulant, low-grade Gellerpox trace-compound, and what Plague Surgeons classify as a "wound-keeping agent" — a biological compound that actively prevents the body's healing response from closing the entry point.

In doctrine, the Cursemite is the installation specialist. It does not cause death directly. It prepares the body to receive further rot. Every Cursemite bite is an open door. The Sludge-Grub, the Eyestinger toxin, and the Gellerpox virus all find Cursemite-prepared wounds significantly more receptive. The Cursemite bites first, and everything else enters through what it has opened.

The door does not open from the inside. The Cursemite opens it from the outside, and it stays open forever.
Vermes Putrefactio / Sludge-Grub

The Blessed Stomach

Carrion processor, sump-breeder, acid-bellied pollutant.

The Sludge-Grub is the largest of the four canonical strains, ranging from eight centimeters to over thirty in the oldest colony specimens. The body is soft-segmented, lacking the rigid chitin of the Cursemite, with a translucent outer membrane that allows internal organs to be observed during feeding — a feature that has made it a subject of particular revulsion in Imperial field reports and particular reverence in Vanguard liturgy.

The digestive system is the defining apparatus. The Sludge-Grub maintains three stomach chambers operating in sequence: the first breaks down organic matter without discrimination; the second processes the result with a concentrated acid capable of dissolving light metals, most polymers, and ceramite bonding agents over extended exposure; the third converts the resultant slurry into a nutrient-dense secretion that feeds colony offspring and, when released into the environment, creates conditions favorable to further infestation.

The Vanguard uses Sludge-Grubs as the colony's logistical heart. While Cursemites wound and Eyestingers disrupt, the Sludge-Grubs process everything that falls — turning the battlefield's dead into fuel for the living infestation. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is clean. Every corpse becomes colony infrastructure. The battlefield is always, eventually, a feeding table. The food supply of an extermination operation includes the extermination team.

Blessed is the slow stomach, for it wastes nothing. All who fall become something better than what they were.
Diptera Empyrea / Eyestinger Swarm

The Blessed Wing

Airborne panic engine, blinding swarm, soft-tissue hunter.

The Eyestinger is the only Mutoid Vermin strain that operates exclusively as a collective. Individual specimens, isolated from the swarm, exhibit drastically reduced behavior complexity. Within a swarm, however, the organisms demonstrate coordinated target-selection, distributed threat-response, and what Mechanicus observers have controversially described as "apparent group cognition."

Each individual Eyestinger carries venom sacs capable of producing a neuro-inflammatory compound targeted specifically at ocular tissue and respiratory mucosa. The primary weapon is not death — it is blinding, respiratory distress, and the sensation, when a swarm is encountered, that the air itself has become an opponent. Troops facing Eyestinger Swarms without adequate respirator equipment report complete combat ineffectiveness within four minutes of contact.

The swarm demonstrates a targeting preference for exposed sensory equipment — optic lenses, vox-grilles, respirator filters, scope apertures. It does not attack these components randomly. It prioritizes them. Whether this represents a form of tactical intelligence or an evolutionary attractor toward the most destructive available targets remains a subject of debate among Plague Surgeons who are not concerned enough about the answer to stop the swarms from doing it.

Blessed are those who make the enemy blind, for the blind warrior swings at saints and shadows with equal certainty.
Daemonicus Mechanica / Glitchling

The Blessed Malfunction

Machine-spoiler, scrap-code carrier, shrine-cogitator parasite.

The Glitchling is the strain that most disturbs the Adeptus Mechanicus, and therefore the strain that most delights the Vanguard's Plague Surgeons. It is not, strictly speaking, purely biological. The Glitchling integrates living tissue and what Mechanicus analysts can only describe as "ambient machine-spirit corruption" — a warp-saturation specifically attuned to the frequencies of cogitator and machine operation rather than organic tissue.

The creature nests in cable runs, data-conduits, and shrine-cogitator housing. It does not eat the cable. It sleeps against it. The proximity of its warp-saturated body is sufficient to introduce what Mechanicus engineers document as "systemic data-corruption," "spontaneous function inversion," and "machine-spirit agitation." In plainer language: the machine begins to fail, and fails in ways that resist standard diagnosis because the cause is biological rather than mechanical. Cogitator arrays produce corrupted output. Weapon systems develop unpredictable cycling. Optical feeds corrupt. Communication arrays invert signals. Power systems become inconsistent.

A weapon held by a soldier operating in Glitchling-infested territory may misfire. It may fail to fire. It may fire at the wrong moment. The soldier cannot know which will happen. This uncertainty is the Glitchling's primary weapon — not the malfunction itself, but the mistrust it installs between the warrior and every tool they depend on. The working Mechanicus classification is "Unclassified Warp-Adjacent Biological Anomaly, Mechanically Interactive." This classification satisfies no one, which the Plague Surgeons find correctly humbling for an institution that believes all things can be categorized.

Blessed is the rust that makes the trigger hesitate. In that hesitation, the Rotfather enters.
