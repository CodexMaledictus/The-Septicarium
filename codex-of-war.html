PREFACE

How to Read This Reckoning

Warhammer 40,000 is a battle game of toy soldiers, dice, and a tape measure. Two armies take turns, chase a mission (usually seizing patches of ground and killing the foe), and after a set number of rounds the higher score wins. Everything below is detail laid over that simple spine.

To keep it painless, two markers recur through the text:

▸ Plain speakThe rule in everyday words — what you actually do at the table.
▸ The Tallyman writesA memory-hook or warning, flavoured to the Vanguard.

Two symbols you'll meet everywhere: a stat of "-" means "none / not allowed," and X+ (e.g. 3+) means "roll that number or higher on a D6 to succeed."

I

Dice, Measuring & the Basics

Army, units, models

You command an army of units. A unit is one or more models (figures) that move and fight as one clump. Anything in your army is friendly; the enemy's is enemy.

▸ Plain speakModels = figures. Units = squads. You act on a whole unit at a time, never a lone model unless a rule says otherwise.

Dice

  • 3+ — roll 3 or higher to pass.
  • 2D6 — roll two dice, add them. D6+1 — roll one, add 1.
  • D3 — roll a D6 and halve it, rounding up (1-2→1, 3-4→2, 5-6→3).

Measuring

All distances are in inches, measurable whenever you like. Unless told otherwise, measure to/from the closest part of a model's base.

Leadership & Battle-shock

Leadership (Ld) is nerve, shown like 6+. Test it by rolling 2D6 and meeting the value. A battle-shock roll is a Leadership roll with teeth — fail and the unit is battle-shocked until it recovers. While shocked it loses Objective Control, can't be targeted by your stratagems, and can't perform actions.

▸ The Tallyman writesFear is a clean-flesh affliction — it loosens a man's grip on the soil beneath him. The Grandfather's gifts know no such tremor. Yet even rotbrothers may falter; hold your nerve and the ground stays yours.

III

Moving Models

Pick up models and reposition them in straight lines and turns, up to the move's maximum. Constants: pass through friendly models but not enemy ones; never leave the table edge; turning on the spot is free.

Coherency — stay a clump

After any move, every model in a multi-model unit must be within 2" of one squadmate and within 9" of all of them.

▸ Plain speakDon't string the squad out like beads on a wire, or you'll lose the stragglers as casualties at end of turn.

Engagement range — "in combat"

Within 2" horizontally (5" vertically) of a model is its engagement range. When your model and an enemy's sit inside each other's, those units are engaged — locked in melee.

▸ The Tallyman writesTwo inches is the reach of a rusted blade. Cross it and you are committed to the embrace. Plague Marines do not mind the embrace.
IV

Shape of a Battle Round

A game runs a handful of battle rounds (usually five). Each round, both players take a full turn. So one battle round = two turns. Your turn is always these five phases:

  1. Command — gain resources, test morale, command-tier tricks.
  2. Movement — reposition; reserves arrive.
  3. Shooting — fire ranged weapons.
  4. Charge — run into melee.
  5. Fight — everyone in melee swings, friend and foe.
▸ Plain speakRemember it as Plan → Move → Shoot → Charge → Smash. Fight is the one phase where the enemy also strikes during your turn.
V

The Command Phase

  1. Gain 1 Command Point (CP). Both players do. CP fuels stratagems.
  2. Battle-shock. Test each of your units that is shocked or at/below half-strength. Fail = battle-shocked.
  3. Command abilities. Use rules that trigger "in the Command phase."
▸ The Tallyman writesCount the dead, the living, the points. The Grandfather is, above all, an accountant — the books must balance before slaughter resumes.
VI

The Movement Phase

Select units one at a time; choose a move type for each (even "stand still").

▸ Plain speakStand still to shoot best. Walk to reposition and still shoot. Advance to rush (no shoot/charge). Fall back only to peel a unit out of a losing fight.

A battle-shocked unit falling back must make a Desperate Escape: a hazard roll per model, then a fresh shock test.

▸ The Tallyman writesThe Gardener loves indirect fire — spitting bile over the wall at men it cannot see. Keep it still, let a brother spot, and the dribble becomes a downpour.
VIII

How an Attack Works

The heart of the game. Shooting and melee use the same four steps. Learn it once, you know both.

First: pick weapons & targets

Shooting targets must be in range, visible, unengaged. Melee targets must be a unit you're engaged with.

Then resolve the dice

  1. Hit roll. D6 per attack; meet BS/WS. Unmodified 6 = critical hit; 1 always misses.
  2. Wound roll. D6 per hit; use the table below. Unmodified 6 = critical wound.
  3. Save roll. The defender rolls per wound vs their armour (worsened by AP) or invuln. Pass = no harm.
  4. Inflict damage. Each failed save costs the target the weapon's Damage. 0 wounds = dead.

The Wound Table

Strength vs ToughnessNeed

▸ Plain speakHit → wound → they save → survivors take damage. The defender always rolls their own saves. AP makes saves harder; invuln ignores AP.

Allocating wounds

The defender chooses who takes damage, but: an already-wounded model must finish first, and CHARACTER models are shielded — they can't be picked until the rank-and-file fall (unless hit by [PRECISION]).

▸ The Tallyman writesThis is why Typhus walks behind the Blightlords. Bolts must chew through ten Terminators of rotted ceramite before they may kiss the Herald. Bodies are the Grandfather's truest armour.
IX

The Charge Phase

  1. Declare. Within 12" of an enemy, not engaged, didn't Advance/Fall Back.
  2. Roll 2D6. That's the max move distance.
  3. Move in. If you can reach engagement range, charge. End closer to a target, engaged if able.

A successful charge grants Fights First for the turn — you swing before non-chargers.

▸ Plain speakThe 2D6 must physically reach within 2" of the foe. A double-1 ("2") can never reach. Charging is a gamble; the prize is striking first.
▸ The Tallyman writesWe do not run; we trudge, inexorable, and 2D6 is the Grandfather deciding how far His mercy carries us. Re-roll a failed charge with a CP if the prize is worth the prayer.
X

The Fight Phase

The bloody one — both players' units fight, even on one player's turn. Order:

  1. Pile In. Each engaged unit shuffles up to 3" to bring more models in.
  2. Fight. Units alternate swinging (see below).
  3. Consolidate. After fighting, move 3" more — stay locked, grab a point, or reach a new foe.

Who swings first

Chargers and units with Fights First strike before everyone. Players then alternate activating one melee unit at a time, starting with the active player. Melee uses the same four-step attack sequence with WS.

▸ Plain speakCharge → strike first. Otherwise you and your opponent take turns activating units. You must fight with everything that can.
XI

Sight & Mortal Wounds

Visibility

To shoot a target you must see it — draw a thin imaginary line from any part of your model to any part of theirs. "Fully visible" means nothing but the target's own body blocks any part of it.

Mortal wounds

Mortal wounds ignore all saves. Each removes 1 wound, one at a time. If an attack deals both normal and mortal damage, resolve normal first.

Hazard rolls

The "this might hurt you" die: roll a D6, on a 1-2 the unit takes 1 mortal wound (3 for a Monster/Vehicle).

▸ The Tallyman writesMortal wounds slip past the rot the way the Void Dragon's light slipped past the Grandfather's gifts — and left the Scar. There is no ceramite for them. Respect them.
XII

Terrain & Cover

CategoryEffect
ExposedCraters, debris. No effect — cross freely.
LightBarricades, low walls. Gives cover, won't block movement.
DenseRuins, woods. Blocks sight, slows big models.

The four sight rules

  • Benefit of Cover — a unit in/behind terrain worsens the shooter's hit by 1.
  • Hidden — infantry in dense terrain that hasn't shot is only seen within 15".
  • Obscuring — sight crossing obscuring terrain is blocked entirely.
  • Solid — no sight through ground-floor gaps under 3" (no peeking through windows).
▸ Plain speakHiding in/behind a ruin makes you harder or impossible to shoot. Dense terrain actually blocks line of sight — use it.

Plunging Fire

Shoot from high ground (terrain 3"+ tall, or a TOWERING model) at a target on the floor and your hit chance improves by 1.

XIII

Objectives

Most missions are won by holding marked points. Have models with OC 1+ near one; at the end of each phase and turn, add up each side's nearby OC. Higher total controls it. Tie = nobody (unless it's been secured, which holds until the foe out-controls you).

▸ Plain speakBodies on points win games. OC is how loudly a unit shouts "ours." A shocked unit shouts nothing.
▸ The Tallyman writesKilling satisfies; standing where the foe wished to stand wins. The Vanguard wins by being immovable — plant the brick, hold the soil, let them break on it.

XV

Leaders & Attached Units

A Character with Leader can join a squad pre-battle to form one attached unit — a single unit thereafter.

  • Shot at, it uses the bodyguards' Toughness; wounds hit the rank-and-file first.
  • It carries all keywords of everyone in it.
  • The leader's buff lasts until the leader dies; the "unit" only counts destroyed when the last original model falls.
▸ The Tallyman writesThe Putrifier rides inside the ten-strong Plague Marine brick aboard Blister. The brick eats the bullets; the Putrifier sweetens their plagues. The Grandfather's arithmetic — one protected, ten emboldened.
XVI

Transports, Monsters & Vehicles

Transports

Carry a set number of models. Embark by ending a move within 3"; disembark in your Movement phase (within 3", or 6" for a riskier Combat Disembark). If destroyed, passengers make an emergency disembark with a hazard roll; any that can't fit are lost.

▸ The Tallyman writesThe Hearse swallows fourteen. Typhus, the Blightlords, and the Lord of Virulence ride together, exactly filling its corroded gut — a rolling reliquary of contagion.

Monsters & Vehicles

  • Move through other (non-M/V) models on normal/advance moves.
  • Can shoot while engaged (close-quarters), at -1 to hit unless using a pistol-type weapon.
  • Shooting at an engaged M/V is allowed at -1 to hit.
  • Many lack a base (FRAME) — measure to/from the closest point of the model itself.
XVII

Reserves, Deep Strike & Flying

Strategic Reserves

Hold units off-table (up to half your points). They arrive via an ingress move — within 6" of a board edge, 8"+ from enemies, usually from round 2. Anything still in reserve after round 3 is destroyed.

Deep Strike

Arrive anywhere 8"+ from enemies — even behind their lines. Teleporting Terminators thrive on it.

Flying

A FLY unit can "take to the skies" when moving: -2" distance, but floats over models and terrain, ignoring vertical climbs.

▸ Plain speakReserves = arrive from an edge later. Deep Strike = drop in anywhere 8"+ away. Fly = float over obstacles.

XIX

Actions & Odds and Ends

Actions

Mission tasks. A unit that starts one usually can't shoot or charge that turn; being shocked or in melee stops it.

Auras

Buffs (or banes) affecting units within a range of the bearer. A model is always in its own aura; two of the same don't stack.

Half-strength

Starting strength = models (or wounds) at the start. "Below half-strength" triggers battle-shock and many army rules — track it.

Destroyed

0 wounds = a model removed; all models gone = unit destroyed. Resolve "when destroyed" rules before removal, after the attacker finishes its attacks.

▸ Plain speakActions tie a unit down. Auras are buff-bubbles. "Half-strength" is the magic phrase for morale and army rules.
