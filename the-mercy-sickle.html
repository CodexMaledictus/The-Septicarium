The Mercy-Sickle

“It does not kill first. It makes the victim understand why death is late.”

The Mercy-Sickle is the hooked relic-blade borne by Mortivus Classarion, plaguebound commander of The Rotfather’s Vanguard. The archive does not classify it as a simple melee weapon. It is a relic, sentence, instructional device, and battlefield punctuation mark. Mortivus does not draw it when the battle begins. He draws it when the battle has learned what it is.

Its name is not kindness. Its name is doctrine. Those who receive its so-called mercy are rarely spared pain, fear, or death. They are spared ignorance. Mortivus appears to believe this is the only mercy worth giving.

This page preserves contradictory provenance rumors, enemy witness fragments, observed effects, and a corrupted schematic of the blade. Do not mistake the schematic for containment. The edge has been known to alter the behavior of those who study it too closely.

Relic Examination

The Hook of Instruction

The outer hook is not shaped for clean decapitation. It is shaped to catch, drag, turn, and expose. Mortivus uses this portion when a target still believes posture, armor, rank, or faith can keep meaning intact.

The hook teaches by interruption. It arrests motion, opens guard, and denies the enemy a clean heroic line. Witnesses often recall the moment the hook catches more vividly than the wound that follows.

Vault Annotation The hook is where the name “mercy” becomes hardest to misunderstand. It does not release. It keeps.
Threat Close execution and symbolic capture.
Doctrine Understanding before termination.
Observed Use Elite targets, priests, officers, oath-holders.
Handling Denied to all clean hands.

Provenance Rumors

Contradiction Preserved

The archive refuses a single origin. The blade benefits from uncertainty.

No verified creation record exists. The Mercy-Sickle appears in war-notes before it appears in armory inventories, and its early mentions conflict in ways that suggest either deliberate concealment, corrupted memory, or a weapon whose history has become infected by its use.

Rumor I

Reforged From an Older Reaper

Some plague-veterans insist the Mercy-Sickle was cut down from a larger reaping weapon borne by an older Death Guard champion whose name Mortivus refuses to speak. The weapon was allegedly too long, too ceremonial, and too proud. Mortivus shortened it, hooked it, and made it intimate.

This version pleases the warband because it suggests inheritance. The archive distrusts it because warriors prefer histories that make their weapons feel earned.

Rumor II

Corpse-Harvester of a Dead Hive

Another account claims the blade began as a corpse-processing tool on a quarantined hive world, used by plague-serfs to drag bodies from sealed habitation stacks. Mortivus found it among millions of dead and recognized that it had already learned the shape of labor.

This rumor explains the blade’s agricultural cruelty: not a duelist’s weapon, but an implement made for harvesting what can no longer object.

Rumor III

A Loyalist Relic Curved by Shame

Enemy fragments suggest the weapon may contain the broken spine of an Imperial relic-blade once used in shrine defense. If true, the sickle is not merely corrupted metal. It is conquered sanctity made to serve a new sermon.

Mortivus has never confirmed this. He has also never denied it when loyalists were present.

Rumor IV

Grown, Not Forged

The least comfortable testimony claims the Mercy-Sickle slowly became itself. It began as one weapon, then altered through battles, prayers, corrosion, and repeated use. The hook deepened. The edge changed. The grip accepted only one hand.

In this account, Mortivus did not find the blade. The blade finished finding him.

Why It Is Called Mercy

Doctrinal Inversion

The name is the cruelest part of the relic.

False Definition Rejected

Mercy is not release.

Mortivus does not call the blade mercy because it ends pain quickly. It often does not. He does not call it mercy because it grants pardon. It grants none. He does not call it mercy because it allows escape. The blade is shaped to prevent exactly that.

To Mortivus, quick death can be wasteful. A clean death leaves the enemy ignorant, and ignorance is a luxury he refuses to grant when the lesson has ripened.

Mortivus’ Definition

Mercy is comprehension before the end.

The Mercy-Sickle is mercy because it denies the victim the comfort of misunderstanding. It arrives after the fortress has failed, after the oath has cracked, after command has lost coherence, after the target has begun to suspect that resistance was not noble so much as late.

The blade gives them a final recognition: that rot was not an interruption of the world, but a truth beneath it. Mortivus appears to believe this recognition is a gift. The archive records no survivor who agreed without damage.

How Mortivus Uses It

Execution Doctrine // Battlefield Grammar

Mortivus does not swing the Mercy-Sickle like a berserker. He uses it like a final argument.

The Mercy-Sickle is rarely the first weapon seen in a battle. Mortivus does not waste it on disorder that has not yet learned what it is. The blade appears when a resistance has already been spiritually softened, when a commander realizes his orders no longer mean what they meant, when a priest hears his own prayer become unfamiliar, or when an elite warrior understands that personal courage cannot repair the collapse around him.

Mortivus uses the weapon with deliberate economy. He does not flail, posture, or indulge in theatrical excess. He advances with the patience of an execution rite. The hook draws the target out of formation. The inner edge opens armor seams. The spine turns aside desperate blows. The chain-ring pulls the blade back into readiness with a small, wet, ceremonial sound.

Most witnesses agree that the worst moment is not the wound. It is the pause before the wound. Mortivus often allows the chosen target to see the blade clearly. In that pause, the victim understands that they have not been selected for death alone. They have been selected as proof.

Use Pattern I

Against Commanders

Mortivus favors the blade against officers who kept their lines intact longer than expected. The Mercy-Sickle removes the commander not only from life, but from the story their soldiers needed them to embody.

Use Pattern II

Against Priests and Oath-Holders

The blade is often reserved for those whose faith sustained others. Mortivus cuts them last enough to make their followers watch and early enough to let despair finish the battle.

Use Pattern III

Against Elite Survivors

When a warrior refuses to break, Mortivus does not always crush them immediately. He uses the blade to teach the difference between endurance and meaning.

Observed Effects

Physical // Psychological // Spiritual

The blade does not wound in one category.

Physical Effect

The Body Opened as Evidence

The Mercy-Sickle causes hooked, dragging trauma unsuited to clean battlefield death. It catches beneath armor lips, pulls through weakened seams, and turns defensive posture into exposure. Wounds made by the inner edge often putrefy rapidly, even when inflicted on subjects whose armor or physiology should resist common corruption.

The blade appears particularly suited for disabling elite targets without immediately removing their awareness. Limbs fail. Breath becomes labor. The body remains present long enough to participate in the lesson.

Psychological Effect

The Pause Before the Cut

Survivors and witnesses frequently remember anticipation more vividly than impact. The blade creates a fixation response. Eyes follow the curve. Hands tighten. Vox discipline breaks. Warriors who have endured artillery, plague-fire, and close assault still report dread when Mortivus lowers the sickle into view.

This fear is not simple fear of pain. It is fear of being personally selected by something patient, intelligent, and already convinced that the victim has something left to understand.

Spiritual Effect

The Soul Counted Before Death

The Mercy-Sickle has repeatedly disrupted prayer, oath-recitation, and command cant. Victims describe feeling counted, weighed, or recognized by the edge. Several enemy accounts claim the blade made them feel judged before it touched them.

The archive does not confirm true sentience. It confirms only that the blade behaves like an object around which meaning becomes dangerous.

Battlefield Witness Accounts

Those who saw the blade used rarely describe the same wound. They describe the same silence.

Final Archive Judgment

Relic Classification Remains Open

The Mercy-Sickle is not merely Mortivus’ blade. It is his doctrine made hand-held.

The relic embodies the central horror of Mortivus Classarion: that death is not always the point, that suffering can be arranged into instruction, and that mercy can be redefined until the victim begs for the older cruelty of simple execution.

Its hook denies escape. Its edge denies ignorance. Its grip denies clean ownership. Its name denies comfort. Wherever it appears, the battle has already entered its final grammar. The enemy may still breathe, fire, pray, and stand, but the Mercy-Sickle announces that Mortivus has begun writing the conclusion.

The archive therefore classifies the weapon as a relic of doctrinal execution, spiritual rupture, and battlefield revelation. It is not the most destructive weapon carried by the Vanguard. It is worse than that. It is the one Mortivus uses when destruction alone would be insufficiently educational.
