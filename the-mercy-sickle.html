The Mercy-Sickle

A spear-hafted plague sickle carried like a final argument.

The Mercy-Sickle is not a farming crescent, nor a berserker’s sweeping scythe. It is a long, execution-polearm: a spear-like haft bearing a shorter, thicker curved blade near the head, built to catch, wrench, expose, and finish. Mortivus does not flourish with it. He advances, arrests resistance, and turns the chosen target into instruction for those still watching.

Primary Form Spear-Hafted Plague Sickle

Long haft. Reinforced collar mount. Short heavy mercy blade. Inner catching bite. Chain-ring retention point. Devotional tags and refusal residue.

Tactical Purpose Selection Before Slaughter

The weapon controls distance, tears a target out of coherence, and makes the wound public enough to destabilize everyone who survives it.

Naming Logic Mercy as Final Clarity

It is called mercy because Mortivus reserves it for the moment when denial must stop pretending it is endurance.

Chamber Warning Do Not Misdescribe the Blade

The curved cutting head is short, thick, and mounted to a polearm. Do not catalogue it as a simple hook or light sickle.

Examination Node // Curved Blade Head

The Thick Curved Mercy Blade

This is the part that gives the weapon its name: not a thin farming sickle, but a short, heavy curved execution blade mounted to a spear-like haft.

Short Blade, Heavy Sentence

The Mercy-Sickle’s blade is shorter and thicker than a common scythe or crescent weapon. It is a dense curved killing head fixed near the end of a long haft, built to bite, catch, and lever armored targets rather than sweep elegantly through open air.

Use
Used at close-to-mid reach to catch armor, punish forward movement, and drag selected enemies out of formation.
Effect
The target feels the weapon’s mass before the edge completes the wound.
Warning
Do not describe the blade as elegant. It is compact, brutal, and deliberately heavy.

Supplementary Plate // Close Edge Memory

The blade edge retains witness pressure more readily than the rest of the weapon. Chamber adepts report that survivors remember the inner curve first, then the pause before the cut, then the realization that the weapon was never drawn for chaos, only for proof.

Origin Cluster // Unstable

Provenance Rumors

No single origin has survived chamber review. The weapon is documented through contradiction, surviving only as rumors strong enough to injure certainty.

Rumor I // The Refusal Harvest

Some records claim the blade head was recast from siege-hooks and execution irons recovered after a devotional purge, then mounted to a plague polearm so that resistance could be caught before it was cut. This would explain the collar’s strain pattern and the oddly functional brutality of the mercy blade.

Rumor II // The Chapel Conversion

Another cluster insists that the weapon was once a ceremonial processional spear used in plague liturgy aboard a rotting warship chapel. The curved blade was added later, thickened and shortened after too many “merciful” executions took too long.

Rumor III // The Weapon That Earned Its Own Name

A minority reading denies formal construction altogether. In that view, the Mercy-Sickle is simply the latest stable shape assumed by a field-tool of recurring meaning, a weapon rebuilt so many times by ritual need that its identity now survives the metal.

Execution Doctrine

How Mortivus Uses It // Why It Is Called Mercy

Mortivus does not swing the Mercy-Sickle like a frenzied reaper. He uses it as a controlled battlefield grammar: selection, arrest, exposure, conclusion.

Against Commanders

The Mercy-Sickle is often reserved for leaders who keep their lines coherent longer than expected. The long haft closes distance without giving away commitment. Once the inner bite catches a target at the shoulder seam, gorget, or weapon harness, the commander’s body becomes the collapse point for everyone watching.

Why “Mercy”

The name is not kindness. It is clarification. Mortivus brings out the Mercy-Sickle when a battlefield has reached the stage where pretense must stop: the chosen target can no longer believe courage, speed, prayer, or authority will repair the collapse around them. The weapon ends debate. That, in Mortivus’ doctrine, is mercy.

Observed Pattern I The haft controls distance so the victim understands they were selected before they are touched.
Observed Pattern II The curved blade catches and turns resistance into cooperation with the wound.
Observed Pattern III Mortivus pauses just long enough for witnesses to comprehend that the target has not been chosen for death alone, but for demonstration.
Observed Consequence Spectrum

What It Does

The Mercy-Sickle does not wound in one category. Survivors consistently describe physical injury, psychological breakage, and spiritual dislocation as one event.

Chamber Reading

The Mercy-Sickle combines weight, reach, leverage, shame, and ritual timing. The victim’s body is caught, their motion betrays them, their audience is forced to witness the transition, and the blade’s compact brutality makes the event feel neither heroic nor chaotic. It feels procedural. That is why the fear lingers.

Those who survive contact often describe the same sequence: first the ring, then the recognition of reach, then the pull, then the sudden intimacy of the curve, and finally the intolerable understanding that the wound was made to be seen.

Physical

  • Catches armor rims, strap work, joints, exposed cabling, or weakened flesh seams.
  • Uses the long haft for leverage, dragging, unbalancing, and controlled tearing.
  • Produces wounds that feel “hooked open” rather than merely cut.
  • Frequently leaves the target upright long enough to be witnessed.

Psychological

  • The ring and pause before impact are remembered more vividly than the strike itself.
  • Witnesses interpret the selection as personal, even if they were not the chosen target.
  • Command structures collapse faster after a Mercy-Sickle demonstration.
  • Survivors report a sense that retreat only deepened the event.

Spiritual

  • The weapon creates the sensation that one’s final certainty was caught before flesh was.
  • Faith-sustained targets often lose symbolic authority before they lose blood.
  • Ritual objects near the impact zone are frequently reinterpreted as failed promises.
  • Some adepts claim the blade “harvests refusal” and stores the residue in chamber air.

Phase I

The Perfumed Refusal // Breach

The first contact occurs in corridors already sweet with ritual corruption. Standard plague-marines are cut down in the early exchange as the enemy’s pace, glamour, and shock assault outstrip expectation.

Short Lore Record

The enemy came lacquered in perfume, silk-light blasphemy, and impossible speed. They shattered the first containment line, tore standard units apart, and moved through the corridor as though pain were choreography. One champion, bright with obscene confidence, reached Mortivus himself and opened a wound deep enough to survive the battle in memory.

What followed gave the weapon one of its strongest names. Mortivus gave ground without yielding shape. He let the champion enter certainty, turned the long haft, fixed the inner bite behind the enemy’s forward motion, and drew the Mercy-Sickle in close. The thick curved blade did not kill the champion quickly. It removed the performance from them first. By the end of the exchange, the corridor no longer smelled like perfume. It smelled like instruction.

Archive Judgment // Blade Chamber Closed

Closing Assessment

The Mercy-Sickle remains under watch because it is more than a weapon and less than a relic in the comfortable sense. It is a disciplined execution-polearm whose shape makes doctrine visible. The long haft extends selection. The short curved blade makes the end feel personal. The ring remembers. The tags apologize.

Any future chamber work should preserve the weapon’s true form: a spear-hafted plague sickle with a compact heavy mercy blade. Do not reduce it to a generic hook. Do not prettify it into a scythe. It is narrower in gesture, uglier in purpose, and far more intimate in use.
