The Mercy He Gave

“The Kin tried to count the cost. Mortivus made the cost remember them.”

This sub-crypt contains the recovered blackbox fragments of a Leagues of Votann claim-force broken under the hand of Mortivus Classarion and The Rotfather’s Vanguard. It is not a Death Guard victory hymn. It is an enemy ledger, damaged but not silent, still trying to speak in the clean language of cost, oath, inheritance, and practical survival.

The Kin did not panic like lesser defenders. They measured. They fortified. They adjusted firing lanes, calculated acceptable losses, invoked ancestral guidance, and treated the plague-host as a problem to be solved. Their first mistake was believing all problems remained obedient to calculation.

Mortivus did not merely defeat them. He entered their logic. He bent their ledgers, their dead, their oath cycles, and the consultation of their Ancestor Core until the surviving records could no longer agree whether the fallen had been preserved, stolen, returned, or made into a lesson.

Enemy File

Leagues of Votann // Kin Record

Who were the Kin?

The enemy encountered here were Kin of the Leagues of Votann: short in stature only to those who mistake scale for endurance. They are engineered, practical, stubborn, and bound by inherited memory. Their wars are not crusades of hysteria. Their campaigns are claims, debts, contracts, survival measures, ancestral obligations, and grudges entered into ledgers with patient seriousness.

The Kin mine, build, salvage, trade, fortify, and endure. They do not rush to superstition when steel fails. They first check the weld, the pressure seal, the ammunition count, the command relay, and the cost of continuing. They are dangerous because they are not easily impressed by terror. They meet horror by measuring it.

This made Mortivus’ victory more useful. A frightened enemy proves little. A calculating enemy proves more. When the Kin began to understand that their calculations were not wrong, but insufficient, the lesson ripened.

The Oathband

Kôrvynd Claim-Oath

They came to claim ore. They left as warning.

The Kôrvynd Claim-Oath entered the contested extraction zone under the authority of a mineral salvage claim older than three local Imperial regimes. To the Kin, the site was not a battlefield at first. It was an asset recovery problem complicated by trespassers, contamination, and hostile interference.

Their force was composed with disciplined practicality: Hernkyn scouts to chart spoil routes and vent access; Hearthkyn line squads to lock the extraction lanes; Brôkhyr engineers to keep the machines obedient; Cthonian Beserks for tunnel shock-action; and the Graven-Aegis Hearthguard to protect the inner mine-vault where the claim’s true value waited under sealed stone.

The first casualty report still reads like a mining accident. The second reads like a raid. The third begins using the term “plague vector.” The fourth stops trusting its own timestamps.

Primary Enemy Character

Kâhl Durnek Vôrr // Holder of the Third Claim

The commander who calculated correctly until the field stopped obeying calculation.

Durnek Vôrr was not reckless. That is why his defeat matters. He did not underestimate the Death Guard as weak, stupid, or slow by accident. He identified them as durable, contamination-heavy infantry with dangerous morale effects and prepared accordingly. He established fallback lanes, layered kill-zones, reserve routes, armored support, and tunnel demolition charges. For the first half of the engagement, his plan worked.

Mortivus lost bodies. The Vanguard bled. Plague marines were buried under mining charges, caught in overlapping volkite fire, and forced through choke points where the Kin punished every meter. Vôrr’s ledger did not fail because he was incompetent. It failed because he measured the wrong war.

Vôrr believed the engagement would be decided by survivability, fire discipline, position, and cost. Mortivus was fighting for the moment when cost itself became sacred, when the Kin would continue because stopping would dishonor the dead, and continuing would deliver more dead into the record. That was the mercy. Mortivus let them understand the trap only after honor had already made retreat impossible.

Disposition Practical, stubborn, respected, slow to anger, difficult to frighten.
Strength Excellent control of fallback geometry, fire lanes, and cost-efficient sacrifice.
Fatal Error Reduced Mortivus to a battlefield problem instead of a doctrinal infection.
Final Record His last command was not retreat. It was “preserve the names.”

Elite Unit Record

The Graven-Aegis Hearthguard

They were not broken easily. That is why Mortivus chose them.

The Graven-Aegis Hearthguard were the finest warriors committed to the inner mine-vault. They wore the heaviest war-plate available to the Kôrvynd Claim-Oath and carried themselves with the stillness of those who had already decided the cost of dying. They were not ceremonial guards. They were practical brutality given oath-form.

Their defensive work was almost perfect. They anchored firing lanes, rotated wounded Kin without disorder, punished the Vanguard’s first close assaults, and held the pressure line while plague fumes filled the vault approaches. Their armor seals degraded, but their formation did not. Their casualty rate became unsustainable, but their oath-cycle continued.

Mortivus waited for that oath-cycle to complete before entering the vault threshold. The archive marks this as deliberate. He did not interrupt their honor. He allowed it to finish, then made it meaningless.

Formation Shielded close-order elite guard protecting the inner vault claim.
Primary Strength Discipline under pressure. Oath cohesion remained intact past predicted collapse.
Confirmed Action Repelled three plague marine pushes before Mortivus personally entered the line.
Final Oath “Hold the claim. Preserve the names. Return what is owed.”

Battle Reconstruction

The Mercy Engagement

The battle did not begin as horror. It began as a claim dispute.

The following reconstruction is assembled from Kin blackbox fragments, Septicarium overlays, corrupted ancestor-query residue, and plague-host after-action traces. The order remains disputed. The meaning does not.

Phase I

The Claim

The Kôrvynd did not enter as pilgrims or zealots. They entered with maps, contracts, extraction tolerances, and ancestral claim authority. The mine-vault beneath the dead ridge contained ore, relic-alloy, and machine-viable mineral seams that Vôrr judged worth the risk of contamination.

Hernkyn scouts marked the spoil routes and found the first signs of previous occupation: corroded Imperial survey pylons, half-buried plague bells, and tunnel doors sealed from the outside with rusted chain. The Kin logged the details as environmental hazard, not omen.

Mortivus let them establish order. He let them believe the site could still be organized.

Final Septicarium Verdict

Mercy Record Preserved

Mortivus did not spare them. He preserved the wound.

The phrase “The Mercy He Gave” does not refer to kindness. It refers to the form of sentence Mortivus chose. He allowed the Kin to remain Kin long enough to watch their strongest habits become instruments of defeat: calculation, oath, memory, cost, preservation, and ancestral return.

Their practicality did not save them. It made the lesson cleaner. Their courage did not save them. It made the offering weightier. Their dead did not vanish. That would have been simple. They remained in the ledger, but the ledger no longer belonged only to them.

This is why the sub-crypt remains attached to Mortivus Classarion’s name. It is not merely a battle. It is a statement of doctrine.
