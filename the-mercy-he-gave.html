ATTACHED SUB-CRYPT // TESTIMONY OF MERCY

The Mercy He Gave

“He did not end their suffering. He taught it to belong to everyone.”

This chamber preserves one of the most infamous atrocity-stains attached to the name of Mortivus Classarion: a brutal engagement against a Leagues of Votann prospecting force whose elite core was broken not only in flesh, but in the meaning of endurance itself.

The archive marks this as the first battle in which Mortivus’ mature doctrine of mercy appears fully formed. He did not simply butcher the enemy. He took the part of their culture that most resembled pride, endurance, inheritance, and kin-memory and made it scream inside its own armor.

Archive Classification GRIMDARK VICTORY STAIN // LEAGUES OF VOTANN CONTACT // SUBJECT: EINHYR HEARTHGUARD
Septicarium Note The chamber does not consider this battle notable because Mortivus won. It considers it notable because of the way he chose to let the enemy remain alive for far too long.

ENGAGEMENT SLATE

BATTLE DISPOSITION

Contact in the Broken Extraction Galleries

The engagement took place in a mineral extraction complex carved deep into a dead world’s crust. The Leagues of Votann had established fortified galleries, hard-sealed transit spines, and ancestral machine cores built to preserve both labor and lineage-memory. Mortivus judged the site ideal: enclosed, disciplined, proud, and full of systems that believed themselves sealed.

ENEMY CATALOGUE

VOTANN PRESENCE

The Kin He Chose to Break

Primary Opposition

Einhyr Hearthguard

Elite Kin clad in exo-armor, slow to panic, difficult to kill, and bred for brutal close-quarter persistence. Mortivus chose them as the core of his lesson because they most closely resembled endurance without rot.

Support Elements

Prospecting Kinhost

Hardened industrial soldiery whose discipline depended on line integrity, sealed corridors, and deep trust in inherited systems. Mortivus treated that trust as an infection route.

Strategic Value

Gene-Lock Archive

More than a data-vault. It preserved ancestral memory, duty chains, and industrial identity. Mortivus recognized immediately that desecrating this chamber would wound the Kin more deeply than destroying a thousand tons of machinery.

RECOVERED NARRATIVE

WITNESS-COLLAPSED ACCOUNT

The Battle Itself

Phase I

The Sealing of Breath

Mortivus did not launch his assault when the first bulkhead fell. He ordered his plague marines to hold the breach and contaminate the extraction galleries instead. Ventilation shafts were seeded. Lift cages were left functional but tainted. Alarm bells were allowed to ring, then quietly fed a wet residue from the censers chained to the vanguard line.

The Kin below trusted their walls. Their rebreathers trusted their filters. Their command lattice trusted inherited calibrations older than some worlds. That trust kept them in place while the first spores crept through the ducts, settled against their armor seams, and began the slow work of making sealed breath feel thick, warm, and wrong.

Phase II

The Hearthguard Hold

When Mortivus entered Gallery II, the Einhyr Hearthguard met him at the transit spine. They were magnificent in the way only stubborn things can be. Armor locked. Shock mauls raised. Rotary fire chewing the darkness into brass and sparks. They did not break when the first of the prospecting kin began vomiting blood behind them. They did not break when bells began to ring from a shaft no longer connected to power.

Mortivus advanced through the fire as though through incense. His armor drank shrapnel. The Mercy-Sickle rose and fell. One Hearthguard lost an arm at the shoulder and kept swinging. Another took a plague-round through the visor, staggered, then crushed one of Mortivus’ own before collapsing in a slurry of marrow and blackened mucus.

The fight became so close that armor seals tore on jagged walls. Kin gauntlets crushed plague marine faceplates. Rusted blades peeled open exo-rig joints. Bolts burst inside torsos. Shock fields turned organs into broth. Mortivus carved the lead Hearthguard from groin to throat and stepped aside as the split halves fell in opposite directions, still twitching inside their own failing systems.

Phase III

The Climax in the Gene-Lock Chamber

The surviving Hearthguard made their final stand around the gene-lock archive. Their commander, a grim Kin in ancestor-marked plate whose name the chamber renders only as Thraim Ironwrit, locked the inner vault and ordered every living defender to die before letting the Death Guard inside.

Mortivus did not answer with argument. He answered by ordering the wounded dragged in. Kin wounded. Death Guard wounded. Mining serfs. Hearthguard not yet dead. He had them piled together before the archive gates while the bells continued tolling overhead. Then he breached the vault not with explosives, but by hammering the Mercy-Sickle into the command seam and letting the rot in his blade infect the lockwork until the machine itself began to weep.

When the gate opened, Thraim Ironwrit charged him. The Hearthguard commander was still strong enough to crack a plague marine’s ribcage in one swing. He struck Mortivus across the helm and split the faceplate open enough for wet vapor to hiss out. Mortivus took the blow, closed the distance, and drove the Mercy-Sickle through the Kin’s abdomen with such force that the tip burst out through the reactor pack. He did not remove it quickly.

He held Thraim there, impaled and breathing, while the archive chamber filled with the stench of opened coolant, split bowel, copper blood, hot fungal bloom, and machine oil. Then Mortivus turned the dying Kin commander toward his own gene-lock vault and split the ancestral core open in front of him. Memory cables snapped like tendons. Archive caskets burst. Lineage slurry spilled across the floor. The Kin around them tried to advance and were cut down at the knees, the throat, the visor, the groin. Those who fell were not granted swift endings.

Phase IV

The Mercy He Gave

Here lies the act for which the chamber was named.

Mortivus ordered the surviving Hearthguard, prospecting kin, and his own wounded gathered together in the archive chamber. He had their helmets removed. He had the dead stacked at the walls. He had ruptured archive canisters poured over the floor so that ancestral nutrient gel mingled with blood, phlegm, molten insulation, and the soft leakage from opened intestines. The room stank of hot metal, rot, and things meant to remain inside bodies.

Then he sealed the chamber.

For hours the living listened to one another breathe. Then cough. Then retch. Then beg. The spores worked through open wounds and exposed mouths. Flesh blistered beneath armor edges. Eyes turned rheumy. Tongues swelled. Nails blackened. Some Kin tore at their own throats trying to clear lungs that had begun growing wet gardens inside them. Others held to discipline until their teeth fell out in blood-slick clumps and their repressed cries became whimpers.

Mortivus did not leave. He remained with them in the dark heat of the sealed chamber, listening. Whenever one tried to crawl away from the pile of suffering bodies, he pushed them gently back with the heel of the Mercy-Sickle. Whenever one begged for death, he denied it. Whenever one tried to curse him, blood and fungus filled their mouth before the words could properly form.

At the end of the long watch, no one in that chamber any longer knew whose pain belonged to whom. Kin lay tangled with plague marines. Ancestor gel clung to opened wounds. The elite Einhyr Hearthguard, paragons of hard endurance, were crying in the same wet, childlike voices as dying miners and gut-shot traitors. That was Mortivus’ mercy. He did not end suffering. He abolished loneliness within it.

Phase V

Aftermath

When the chamber was finally opened, the scene no longer resembled victory in the common sense. It resembled an answered prayer from the underside of creation. Some Kin were dead with mouths frozen open in disbelief. Some still lived but no longer possessed enough intact selfhood to know what they had once guarded. Several plague marines rose from the pile giggling through broken teeth, their armor plated with congealed ancestor slurry.

Mortivus took what remained of the gene-lock core and had it mounted as a relic within the lower sanctums of The Rotfather’s Mercy. The battle was entered into the tally not as conquest, but as demonstration.

CENSURED APPENDIX

UNNECESSARY DETAIL

The Chamber’s Most Disturbing Detail

The account above is already sufficient, but one additional detail was marked with special disgust even by those who serve Nurgle. It is sealed here for completeness.

When the archive chamber was opened, Thraim Ironwrit was still alive. Mortivus had left him upright against the burst gene-lock core, impaled through the abdomen and pinned there by the Mercy-Sickle’s lower hook. His legs were gone below the knee where rot, arterial loss, and crushing collapse had made a single ruin of them. His exo-armor had split at the seams and the flesh inside had swollen, blistered, and sagged into the nutrient gel on the floor.

Yet what horrified the witnesses was not survival alone. It was that the dying Kin commander had spent the final stretch of his life cradling the head of one of his own Hearthguard, rocking it gently as if it were a sleeping child, while both of them drowned in the warm slurry of opened ancestry and pooled blood.

Mortivus reportedly stood over the scene and said only this: “Now your endurance has become communal. This is the mercy you denied yourselves.”

SUB-CRYPT CLOSING

ENTRY PERSISTS

Why the Chamber Remains Open

The Septicarium preserves this atrocity not because it is excessive, but because it is clarifying. It reveals Mortivus’ mature doctrine more precisely than any battlefield summary. He does not seek only to kill. He seeks to corrupt the meaning of endurance, kinship, inheritance, and comfort.

Where others offer mercy as release, Mortivus offers it as forced participation in shared ruin. That is why this chamber remains bound to his name.
