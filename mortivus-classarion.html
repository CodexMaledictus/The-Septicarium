Mortivus Classarion

“Blessed by Nurgle. Unfinished by design.”

Mortivus Classarion is the plaguebound commander of The Rotfather’s Vanguard, a Plague Champion of uncertain station whose record refuses stable classification. Some name him Lord of Contagion. Some place him among Blightlord elites. A few censured fragments suggest proximity to stations not meant for open record.

The Septicarium confirms only this: Mortivus is cunning, occult, biologically patient, spiritually corrosive, and drawn toward daemonhood by a private plague-prophecy whose origin remains hidden, infected, or deliberately unfinished.

ORIGIN THREADS

CONTRADICTION PRESERVED

The Name Before the Rot

The Dossier refuses a single origin for Mortivus Classarion. This is not considered failure. It is considered honesty.

One thread claims he was once a lesser champion inside a plague company that bled itself dry during a campaign of prolonged attrition. Another claims he emerged from a shattered boarding force whose officers died faster than their orders did. A third insists he never properly rose at all, but simply continued issuing commands after every superior authority had become corpse, vector, or shrine.

All threads agree on the same trait: Mortivus read decay better than others read maps. Where commanders saw stalled offensives, he saw ripening pressure. Where others counted casualties, he counted openings. Where lesser champions prayed for blessing, Mortivus studied the exact moment when belief, flesh, and command all began to soften together.

EARLY ASCENT

ROOKIE TO CHAMPION

How He Rose

Mortivus did not rise by spectacle. He rose by recurrence. He carried out hopeless advances without complaint, returned with details others had failed to notice, and remembered exactly how enemy morale collapsed by caste, creed, and rank.

He noted which priests lied first, which commanders overused purity rites, which elite troops mistook discipline for invulnerability, and which fortresses depended on ritual routine more than actual strength. By the time others noticed his ambition, he had already turned observation into doctrine.

His earliest followers did not gather because he promised glory. They gathered because battles around him began to feel inevitable. Strongholds did not merely fall. They appeared to discover they had been falling for weeks.

HISTORY OF COMMAND

SELECTED DEVELOPMENT

From Plaguebound Aspirant to Warhost Master

First Noted Pattern

The Ledger of Refused Retreats

Mortivus first enters usable record during a campaign in which withdrawal orders repeatedly failed to reach his position. Whether this was coincidence, enemy interference, or early doctrinal sabotage is unresolved. He held the line long enough for the enemy’s relief force to become trapped inside its own quarantine perimeter.

Rise Among the Plaguebound

The Seven Bell Advance

In one disputed engagement he forbade the assault until seven corrupted bell tones had carried across the field. Each bell matched a different collapse: supply, sanitation, morale, command, liturgy, ammunition discipline, and finally hope. When the advance began, resistance had already become ceremonial.

Formation Pressure

The Rotfather’s Vanguard Coheres

The host that became The Rotfather’s Vanguard did not form through clean oath. It gathered around Mortivus like rot around warmth. Survivors, plague marines, failed rivals, blighted specialists, and lesser champions entered his orbit because his campaigns produced recurrence rather than mere victory.

Current Condition

The Unfinished Apotheosis

Mortivus now commands as one who believes every campaign is part of a petition. Whether he seeks daemonhood, is being led toward it, or is already being rewritten by it remains unresolved. The Dossier records only increasing pressure.

ARMOR RECORD

PANOPLY DISPUTED

The Unresolved Panoply

Some witnesses describe rotted power plate layered with devotional corrosion, horned vents, chain-hung censers, cracked lenses, and scripture cut into rust. Others insist he moves in a heavier silhouette altogether: terminator mass, shrine-posture, and the slow weight of an ambulatory reliquary.

The Septicarium does not resolve this. The armor may have changed. The witnesses may have failed. The warp may have made both accounts true. The most consistent features are the bells, the tally scars, and the sense that the panoply is not merely worn but inhabited.

IDEOLOGY

OCCULT ROT DOCTRINE

What Mortivus Believes

Mortivus does not view rot as mere ruin. He views it as proof that all false separation eventually fails. Flesh, oath, wall, memory, empire, and soul all pretend to be sealed. Nurgle teaches that nothing remains sealed.

His doctrine begins with spiritual decay. Certainty is broken first. Biology follows. Command structure becomes a host. An army may still have guns and armor and yet already be dead if it has begun to doubt the meaning of firing them.

This makes Mortivus dangerous even among the Death Guard. He does not merely endure. He interprets. He does not simply infect. He studies what infection reveals.

WEAPONS AND RELICS

ARMAMENT CLASSIFICATION

The Tools That Follow His Name

Primary Relic

The Mercy-Sickle

A plague-cleaver or manreaper analogue depending on witness quality, curved as if made to harvest not flesh first but refusal. Victims are often described as falling quiet before they cease moving.

Secondary Armament

The Rite-Spitter

A corrupted bolt-weapon or plague-belcher hybrid bound with censer-work. Its physical trauma is less memorable than the fever, hallucination, and liturgical confusion that follow impact.

Occult Implement

The Tally Chain

A chain of plague-marked seals, shell casings, bone shards, and nameplates. Mortivus touches it before sparing, killing, retreating, or ordering contaminations whose meaning becomes apparent only much later.

ABILITIES

OBSERVED EFFECTS

What the Dossier Attributes to Him

Mortivus is not classified as a pure sorcerer by stable record, yet battlefield accounts repeatedly assign occult consequences to his presence. Vox channels sour. Enemy catechisms mispronounce themselves. The wounded become calm at moments when panic would be more rational.

The safest explanation is that he combines Death Guard resilience, disciplined attrition, plagueborn physiology, and Nurgle-favored symbolism so effectively that witnesses mistake the result for witchcraft.

The unsafe explanation is that the witnesses are correct.

BATTLE TACTICS

THEORY OF RUIN

How Mortivus Makes War

Mortivus favors pressure over speed. He permits an enemy to keep ground if holding that ground deepens infection, exhausts command discipline, or convinces the defenders that survival remains possible.

His assaults often follow the same structure: isolate belief, contaminate routine, interrupt command, let fear become procedure, then advance only when resistance has already curdled into ritual.

The Rotfather’s Vanguard under Mortivus is not simply a plague force. It is an argument: all resistance is temporary, all purity is theatrical, and all fortresses are already bodies.

VICTORIES AND DEFEATS

CENSURED BATTLE MEMORY

Selected Campaign Stains

Victory

The Mournbell Boarding

Mortivus boarded a shrine-transport and contaminated the bell array rather than the main chapel. The defenders opened their own sanctuary believing they were answering liturgy.

Victory

The Coughing of Karthax Minor

A fortified moon collapsed without decisive bombardment. Water rites, corpse disposal patterns, and ration chants were contaminated until the garrison continued military posture long after spiritual defeat.

Defeat

The Pallid Reversal

Mortivus once mistook enemy withdrawal for collapse and walked the Vanguard into a prepared kill field. He survived, denied no failure, and later used the names of the dead as planning headings for future wars.

Defeat

The Unanswered Seventh Bell

In one campaign the predicted final collapse never came. Enemy morale held beyond calculation. Mortivus withdrew rather than waste the host. The Dossier marks this as a rare instructional failure.

Restricted

Strife-Locked Fragments

Confirmed references to the Strife System remain limited to battle-record allusion only. The chamber does not expand the subject beyond those engagements.

Omen

The Name Written Before Battle

Several victories attributed to Mortivus appear to have been entered into tally before the battles occurred. The chamber declines to explain this.
