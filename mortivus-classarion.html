Mortivus Classarion

“Blessed by Nurgle. Unfinished by design.”

Mortivus Classarion is the plaguebound commander of The Rotfather’s Vanguard, a Plague Champion of uncertain station whose records refuse stable classification. Some name him Lord of Contagion. Some place him among Blightlord elites. A few censured fragments imply proximity to rites not meant for common record.

The Septicarium confirms only this: Mortivus is cunning, occult, biologically patient, spiritually corrosive, and drawn toward daemonhood by a private plague-prophecy whose origin remains either hidden, infected, or deliberately unfinished.

ORIGIN THREADS

CONTRADICTION PRESERVED

The Name Before the Rot

The Dossier refuses a single origin for Mortivus Classarion. This is not administrative failure. It is considered part of the record.

One origin-thread claims he was once a lesser champion within a plague company that broke away after a disastrous campaign of slow attrition, where Mortivus alone understood that the failure had not been defeat but instruction. Another suggests he rose from a Blightlord cohort whose command structure collapsed during a boarding action aboard an Imperial shrine-vessel. A third, more doubtful strand claims he was never promoted at all, but simply continued issuing orders after every superior had become either corpse, vector, or shrine.

All versions agree on the same pattern: Mortivus survived by reading decay better than others read maps. Where commanders saw stalled assaults, he saw ripening pressure. Where others counted losses, he counted openings. Where lesser champions prayed for favor, Mortivus studied the exact moment when faith, flesh, and command all began to soften.

EARLY ASCENT

ROOKIE TO CHAMPION

How He Rose

Mortivus did not rise by speed, spectacle, or madness. He rose by endurance and memory. The first recorded accounts mark him as a quiet executioner of contaminated orders, a warrior who carried out hopeless advances without complaint and returned with details no one else had thought to collect.

He noted which enemy prayers became frantic first. He remembered which officers lied about casualty reports. He studied which kinds of fear spread faster through infantry, clergy, transhuman elites, and mutant auxiliaries. By the time others noticed his ambition, he had already turned observation into doctrine.

His earliest followers did not gather because he promised glory. They gathered because battles around him began to feel inevitable. Fortresses did not simply fall. They seemed to realize they had been falling for weeks.

HISTORY OF COMMAND

SELECTED DEVELOPMENT

From Plaguebound Aspirant to Warhost Master

First Noted Pattern

The Ledger of Refused Retreats

Mortivus first appears in usable records during a campaign where withdrawal orders repeatedly failed to reach his position. Later analysis suggests the vox errors were real. Later still, the Septicarium questioned whether the errors had been permitted. Mortivus held the line long enough for the enemy’s relief force to become trapped inside its own quarantine perimeter.

Rise Among the Plaguebound

The Seven Bell Advance

In one disputed engagement, Mortivus ordered no charge until seven corrupted bell tones had echoed across the battlefield. Each bell coincided with a different collapse: supply, morale, command, sanitation, liturgy, ammunition discipline, and finally hope. When the advance began, resistance had already become ceremonial.

Formation Pressure

The Rotfather’s Vanguard Coheres

The warhost that became The Rotfather’s Vanguard did not form through a clean oath ceremony. It collected around Mortivus like infection around heat. Survivors, plague marines, failed rivals, oath-rotted specialists, and lesser champions entered his orbit because his campaigns produced something more valuable than victory: recurrence.

Current Condition

The Unfinished Apotheosis

Mortivus now commands as one who believes every campaign is part of a greater petition. Whether he seeks daemonhood, is being led toward it, or is being used as bait by powers beyond his comprehension remains unresolved. The Dossier records only increasing pressure.

ARMOR RECORD

PANOPLY DISPUTED

The Unresolved Panoply

Mortivus’ armor is one of the Dossier’s most persistent contradictions. Some witnesses describe rotted power plate thick with devotional corrosion, horned vents, cracked lenses, chain-hung censers, and layers of scripture scratched into rust. Others insist he moves in a heavier silhouette: terminator mass, Blightlord density, corpse-shrine posture, and a gait like a walking reliquary.

The Septicarium does not resolve this. The armor may have changed. The witnesses may have failed. The warp may have made both accounts true. The most consistent details are the bells, the cracked plague halo behind the helm, and a breastplate marked with tally-wounds that appear deeper after every major engagement.

Internal designation: The Unresolved Panoply. Not because the armor lacks identity, but because its identity is still working.

IDEOLOGY

OCCULT ROT DOCTRINE

What Mortivus Believes

Mortivus does not view rot as mere destruction. To him, decay is proof that all false separation eventually fails. Armor, flesh, oath, wall, empire, memory, and soul all pretend to be sealed. Nurgle teaches that nothing is sealed forever.

His doctrine begins with spiritual decay. Break certainty first. Then biological corruption finds easier passage. Then command structure becomes a host. An army that still has guns may already be dead if it has begun to doubt the meaning of firing them.

This makes Mortivus dangerous even among the Death Guard. He does not merely endure. He interprets. He does not simply infect. He studies what infection reveals.

WEAPONS AND RELICS

ARMAMENT CLASSIFICATION

The Tools That Follow His Name

Primary Relic

The Mercy-Sickle

A plague-cleaver or manreaper-like weapon depending on the account, curved as if made to harvest both flesh and certainty. Wounds caused by the blade are described as strangely quiet. Victims often stop screaming before they stop moving, as though pain has been translated into acceptance.

Sidearm

The Rite-Spitter

A corrupted bolt weapon recorded in several forms: combi-bolter, plague-belcher, or rusted sidearm bound with censers. Its ammunition is less consistent than its effect. Survivors report impact trauma followed by devotional hallucination, fever, and the scent of wet iron bells.

Occult Implement

The Tally Chain

A chain of plague-marked tokens, broken oath seals, teeth, shell fragments, and corroded nameplates. Mortivus has been observed touching it before refusing retreats, sparing enemies, or ordering contaminations that seemed tactically unnecessary until much later.

ABILITIES

OBSERVED EFFECTS

What the Dossier Attributes to Him

Mortivus is not classified as a sorcerer by stable record, yet battlefield accounts repeatedly assign occult consequences to his presence. Vox channels sour. Enemy catechisms mispronounce themselves. Wounded soldiers become calm at moments when panic would be more rational.

The safest interpretation is that Mortivus combines Death Guard resilience, plagueborn physiology, disciplined tactical patience, and Nurgle-favored symbolism so effectively that witnesses describe the result as witchcraft.

The unsafe interpretation is that the witnesses are correct.

BATTLE TACTICS

THEORY OF RUIN

How Mortivus Makes War

Mortivus favors pressure over speed. He will allow an enemy to hold ground if holding that ground deepens their infection, exhausts their command discipline, or convinces them that survival remains possible.

His assaults often follow a pattern: isolate belief, contaminate routine, interrupt command, let fear become procedure, then advance when resistance has become an empty ritual.

The Rotfather’s Vanguard under Mortivus is therefore not simply a plague force. It is an argument: that all resistance is temporary, all purity is theatrical, and all fortresses are already bodies.

VICTORIES AND DEFEATS

CENSURED BATTLE MEMORY

Selected Campaign Stains

Victory

The Mournbell Boarding

Mortivus led a boarding action against a shrine-transport whose defenders had sealed themselves behind layered purity wards. He did not breach the chapel first. He contaminated the bell system. By the time the doors opened, the defenders believed the bells were calling them to procession.

Victory

The Coughing of Karthax Minor

A fortified moon fell without decisive bombardment. Mortivus targeted water rites, ration chants, and corpse disposal schedules. The garrison maintained military posture for six days after spiritual defeat, which the Dossier considers an impressive but irrelevant delay.

Defeat

The Pallid Reversal

Mortivus once misread enemy retreat as collapse. It was bait. The Vanguard suffered losses in a killing field prepared with rare discipline. Mortivus survived, ordered no denial of failure, and later used the names of the dead as tactical headings in future plans.

Defeat

The Unanswered Seventh Bell

In one campaign, the expected seventh collapse never occurred. Enemy morale held beyond prediction. Mortivus withdrew rather than waste the host. This remains one of the few records where the Dossier admits surprise.

Unresolved

The Strife-Locked Fragments

References to the Strife System remain restricted to confirmed battle records only. The Dossier preserves several scars from those engagements, but does not treat that system as its own mythology.

Omen

The Name Written Before Battle

Several victory tallies attributed to Mortivus appear to have been logged before the battles they describe. The Septicarium refuses to identify this as prophecy, clerical failure, or warp contamination.

CENSURED CHAMBER

DISTURBING FRAGMENT

The Mercy He Gave

This section belongs here as a warning, but later it should become its own Rot Scripture page. Mortivus needs one story so disturbing that visitors understand why his followers do not speak of his mercy as kindness.

The chapel had survived the shelling. That was the mistake. Its roof still held. Its saints still stared downward through cracked glass. Its congregation still believed survival meant judgment had passed them by.

Mortivus entered without haste and ordered no slaughter. Instead, he commanded the doors sealed from the outside and the wounded carried in. Enemy wounded. Vanguard wounded. Civilians. Priests. One by one, all were placed beneath the same broken saints.

For seven hours the chapel heard only breathing. Then coughing. Then prayer. Then apology. By dawn, the survivors no longer knew whose pain belonged to whom. Mortivus called this mercy: not the ending of suffering, but the removal of loneliness from it.

The Dossier marks this as the first confirmed expression of Mortivus’ mature doctrine. He does not merely corrupt flesh. He corrupts the meaning of comfort.
