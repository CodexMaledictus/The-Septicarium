The Plaguebound Annals

Where the warband is lowered into memory by chain, wound, oath, and rust.

The Plaguebound Annals are not read. They are descended into. Beneath the Command Deck, the sealed record-vaults breathe through brass vents, plague-censers, and the slow scrape of chains dragged across wet iron.

Commanders are pressed into subject-script. Infantry are counted by footfall and contagion. Engines are recorded by hunger. Relics are weighed by wounds that still remember touch. Battles are embalmed until the field speaks again.

No clean inventory survives the Annals. Every record must rot into doctrine before the vault accepts it.

Before the First Door // Archive Threshold

The Ship Learned Separation Through Loss.

The Annals are not arranged for ease of reading. They are arranged by what each record can survive.

A face must be caged differently than a weapon. A machine must be chained differently than a squad. A battle must be embalmed differently than a doctrine. Early records were once laid together beneath the same bell, and the ship remembers what happened when they touched.

Names began altering casualty ledgers. Relics appeared inside battle reports that had never seen them. Engines were heard turning beneath pages written centuries apart. Since then, every chamber has kept its own kind of rot.

Threshold Law

A record is not placed where it belongs. It is placed where it can do the least damage while still remaining useful.

The first door does not open until the witness understands why the lower vaults are divided.

Subject-Scriptorium // dangerous faces under sealed decision
I // Subject-Scriptorium

Where Dangerous Faces Are Pressed Into Script

The Subject-Scriptorium hangs beneath the Annals like a cage of blue-black glass. The chamber is not reserved for the famous. It is reserved for those whose names alter nearby records.

Mortivus Classarion is kept here because his presence changes the meaning of the formations around him. Plague surgeons, tally-keepers, putrifiers, champions, rivals, and enemy warlords are admitted only when the Annals can prove that their face has become a battlefield force.

Every subject file begins as a containment measure. The name is written once. Then chained. Then watched for movement.

The chamber rejects biography. It accepts only containment, influence, office, mutation, rivalry, and proof that the face changes the records around it.
Muster-Vaults // infantry doctrine counted by pressure
II // Muster-Vaults

Where the Marching Dead Are Counted by Footfall

The Muster-Vaults do not count bodies as numbers. They count the pressure of an advance. Plague Marines enter the vault as walking doctrine. Poxwalkers enter as proof that death can still be mobilized.

Every infantry record asks what the formation does to the ground beneath it. Does it anchor? Does it rot? Does it drown the enemy in patience? Does it make the corridor feel narrower simply by entering it?

The Annals keep infantry as processions, not squads. Each one is a moving chapel of corrupted purpose.

Hanging Muster Records
Plague Marines Poxwalkers Icon Bearer Plague Champion Infantry Procession Rites
The Muster-Vault accepts no roster. It accepts footfall, ground pressure, weapon rhythm, contagion density, and proof that the line changed the field before victory was counted.
Oath-Iron Crypts // sealed judgments inside armor
III // Oath-Iron Crypts

Where the Sealed Judgments Wait in Armor

The Terminator Orders are kept behind doors so heavy the hinges have learned to groan like lungs. Their records are not written as elite honors. They are written as sentences.

Blightlord cadres become anvils of refusal. Deathshroud become the shadow cast by command. These are not faster warriors. They are proximity made lethal, patience wrapped in oath-iron, and execution given ceremonial weight.

The Oath-Iron Crypts preserve the moments when retreat becomes impossible because the armored dead have already arrived.

Hanging Oath-Iron Records
Blightlord Terminators Deathshroud Terminators Mortivus' Personal Guard Named Oath-Cadres
The Oath-Iron Crypt accepts pressure, proximity, oath-weight, bodyguard gravity, and the moment fear realizes it cannot outrun what is already too close.
Engine-Crypts // hunger with armor around it
IV // Engine-Crypts

Where War Machines Are Fed, Chained, and Released

The Engine-Crypts are never quiet. Something always drips. Something always turns. Something always tests its chain against the floor.

Here the Annals keep the engines not as vehicles, but as diseased appetites with armor around them. Crawlers, drones, haulers, helbrutes, brigands, and transports are preserved by the kind of hunger they bring to war.

The engine record must describe what the machine does to sound, pressure, sightlines, morale, and the bodies unfortunate enough to stand in its path.

Hanging Engine Records

The Engine-Crypt rejects vehicle ledgers. It accepts appetite, chain-stress, kill-pressure, shrine-mass, smoke-behavior, and the sound of machinery remembering hunger.
Reliquary // instruments restrained by witness law
V // Reliquary of Contagion

Where Instruments Are Weighed by the Wounds They Keep

The Reliquary does not store objects. It restrains them. Every blade, bell, injector, icon, plague vessel, and corrupted mechanism is kept under watch because function alone cannot explain what it has done.

A relic earns its place when the wound it made refuses to become history. The Mercy-Sickle is not merely Mortivus' weapon. It is a doctrine with an edge. A plague bell is not merely rung. It teaches the field how to decay in rhythm.

The Reliquary records instruments by touch, residue, sound, and consequence.

Hanging Relic Records
The Mercy-Sickle Plague Bells Blight Grenades Icons of Contagion Biological Plague Vessels Corrupted Wargear
The Reliquary accepts residue, handling warnings, wound-memory, ritual use, sound, and the testimony of those who touched the instrument and changed afterward.
War-Chronicles // fields embalmed until they speak
VI // War-Chronicles

Where Battles Are Embalmed Until the Field Speaks Again

The War-Chronicles do not receive every clash. They receive only those fields that continue speaking after the bodies have cooled.

A battle record enters this chamber through evidence: wreckage, enemy testimony, broken bells, dead engines, failed charges, impossible survivals, grudges, reversals, and fragments of strategy that became myth through suffering.

The Pallid Reversal is kept here because it did not merely describe a defeat. It proved that even Mortivus Classarion could be corrected by a field made clever enough to hate him.

The War-Chronicles accept only evidence that remains warm: wreckage, witness, reversal, consequence, humiliation, triumph, and fields that continue speaking after the last shot.
Unwritten Chain // hooks without names
VII // The Unwritten Chain

Where Future Records Wait Before They Have Names

The final chamber is not blank. It is restrained. Empty hooks hang from its ceiling, each one waiting for a unit, relic, enemy, battle, or failure heavy enough to earn a place in the Annals.

This is where future research waits without cluttering the Command Deck. No record is forced. No old battle is dragged here without purpose. The chain remains open until the warband gives it something worth holding.

The Unwritten Chain is the promise that The Septicarium will grow by evidence, not filler.
